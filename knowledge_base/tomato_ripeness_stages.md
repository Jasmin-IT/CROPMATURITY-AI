# Tomato ripeness stages
Source: USDA tomato ripeness colour classification (six stages). Verify and cite the exact edition.

## The six standard colour stages
Tomatoes are commonly classified into six stages: green, breaker, turning, pink, light red and red.
Green: the surface is completely green. Breaker: a definite break in colour from green to tan-yellow, pink or red on no more than 10% of the surface.
Turning: more than 10% but no more than 30% of the surface shows tan-yellow, pink, red or a combination.
Pink: more than 30% but no more than 60% of the surface shows pink or red.
Light red: more than 60% but no more than 90% of the surface shows pink-red or red.
Red: more than 90% of the surface is red.

## Mature green versus immature green
A mature green tomato is fully grown and will ripen after picking. An immature green tomato is still growing and will not ripen well off the vine.
Mature green fruit usually shows a glossy skin, a gelatinous gel around the seeds, and seeds that are not cut when the fruit is sliced.

## Colour is not the only sign
Firmness and aroma also change as fruit ripens. Colour development depends on temperature and variety, so colour alone can mislead.

# Tomato overview and types
Source: University extension crop guides (for example Hawaii CTAHR field tomato guidelines, Iowa State, UC IPM). Verify against a current local extension guide before citing.

## What a tomato is
The tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) belongs to the nightshade family, Solanaceae, together with potato, pepper and eggplant. Botanically the fruit is a berry, but it is used as a vegetable in cooking. Tomato flowers are self-pollinating. Wind or gentle vibration helps release the pollen onto the stigma, and sticky pollen in very humid weather can reduce pollination.

## Determinate and indeterminate plants
Determinate (bush) plants grow to a limited height and set most of their fruit over a short period of a few weeks, then decline. They suit a single large harvest, for example for processing.
Indeterminate (vine) plants keep growing and flowering until frost or disease stops them. They need staking or trellising and are picked repeatedly over many weeks, so one plant carries fruit of every maturity stage at the same time.
Semi-determinate plants sit between the two.

## Fruit types
Common market types are cherry and grape tomatoes (small, often eaten raw), plum or paste tomatoes such as Roma (firm flesh, used for sauce and paste), slicing or globe tomatoes (general purpose), beefsteak tomatoes (large, juicy) and heirloom varieties (old open-pollinated types, often with little disease resistance). Processing tomatoes are bred for machine harvest and uniform ripening.

## Colour when ripe
Red is the most common ripe colour, but ripe tomatoes can also be pink, yellow, orange, green or purple-brown depending on the variety. The standard ripeness colour stages describe red varieties. Unusual varieties can look "unripe" or "semiripe" to a colour-based system even when they are fully ripe.

## Disease resistance codes in seed catalogues
Letters after a variety name show built-in resistance: V for Verticillium wilt, F (or FF, FFF) for Fusarium wilt races, N for root-knot nematodes, T for tobacco mosaic virus, A for Alternaria (early blight) and TSWV for tomato spotted wilt virus. A label such as VFN means resistance to Verticillium, Fusarium and nematodes.

## Days to maturity
Seed catalogues list days to maturity, which differs widely between varieties. Real timing also depends on temperature, light, water and nutrition, so it is only a rough guide.
