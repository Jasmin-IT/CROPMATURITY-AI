## Harvesting
Pick with the calyx attached where possible, handle gently and avoid dropping fruit. Harvest in the cooler part of the day.
Keep picked fruit in the shade and use clean, shallow containers so fruit is not crushed.

## Sorting and storage
Sort by ripeness stage, so that ripe fruit goes to sale first and less ripe fruit can finish ripening.
Avoid storing unripe tomatoes at low temperatures. Cold storage below roughly 10 to 12 degrees Celsius can cause chilling injury and poor colour development.

# Harvesting and handling practices
Source: UC Davis Postharvest Technology Center; university extension post-harvest handling guides. Verify before citing.

## Picking
Pick in the cooler part of the day. Handle fruit gently, because bruises and cuts speed up decay. Do not drop fruit or pile it deep, and use clean, shallow containers so fruit at the bottom is not crushed.

## Protect from sun and heat
Keep picked fruit in the shade. Direct sun on picked fruit raises its temperature and can cause sunscald and quick quality loss.

## Hygiene
Clean tools and containers. Avoid handling plants when the leaves are wet, because this spreads leaf diseases. Remove and dispose of rotten fruit instead of leaving it in the field.

## Sort by ripeness
Sort and pack fruit of the same maturity together. Ripe fruit goes to sale first, and less ripe fruit can finish ripening. Remove fruit with cracks, rot or insect damage.

## Cool quickly
Rapid cooling soon after harvest is important for keeping quality, but do not cool unripe tomatoes below the chilling limit. See the postharvest storage document.

## Reading the readiness score
In this system, "Ready to harvest" means a harvest readiness of 85% or more, "Approaching harvest stage" 60% to under 85%, "Still developing" 30% to under 60%, and "Not ready" below 30%. These are indicators of the current maturity mix. Local market needs and weather should guide the final decision.
