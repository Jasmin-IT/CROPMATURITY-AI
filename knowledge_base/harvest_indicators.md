# Harvest indicators for tomato
Source: UC Davis Postharvest Technology Center (tomato recommendations); university extension harvest guides; USDA tomato colour stages. Verify before citing.

## Skin colour
Skin colour is the main indicator. The six standard stages are green, breaker, turning, pink, light red and red, and they are defined by how much of the surface has changed colour. See the ripeness stages document for the percentages.

## Mature green or immature green
A mature green tomato is full size and will ripen after picking. Signs include a glossy skin and well-developed gel in the seed cavities, and seeds that are not cut when the fruit is sliced. In commercial practice, the minimum harvest maturity is judged from the internal structure of sample fruit. Immature green fruit stays hard and ripens poorly.

## Firmness and feel
Ripe fruit gives slightly under gentle pressure, while unripe fruit is hard. Very soft, wrinkled fruit is past its best.

## Shoulder and calyx
A green or yellow shoulder, or a tight green calyx, suggests the fruit is not fully ripe. Some varieties and hot weather leave a yellow shoulder even on otherwise ripe fruit.

## Which stage to pick
The right stage depends on the market. Fruit for long-distance shipping is usually picked from mature green to breaker or turning and ripened under controlled conditions. Fruit for local sale can stay on the plant until pink to light red. Fruit for sauce or processing is generally left until red. Fruit picked at or after the breaker stage usually develops good colour, while fruit picked before it may colour poorly.

## Picking pattern
Tomatoes ripen unevenly, so several pickings are normal rather than one harvest. A field is usually judged ready when most of the fruit has reached the target stage for the intended market, not when every fruit is ripe.

## Fruit past its peak
Very soft, wrinkled or deep dull red fruit is past its peak. It spoils quickly and can spread decay to other fruit, so it is picked first or removed. This monitoring system does not detect an over-mature stage.

## Things that can mislead colour
Yellow or green shoulders, blotchy ripening, sunscald and varieties that are not red can all make a fruit look less ripe or more ripe than it really is. Strong shade, dim light and hot weather that stops red colour can change what a camera sees.
