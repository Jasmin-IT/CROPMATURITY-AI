## Growth stages
Tomato growth moves from seedling to vegetative growth, flowering, fruit set, fruit enlargement and ripening.
After fruit reaches full size it enters the mature green stage and then begins to change colour.

## Ripening
Ripening is driven partly by the plant hormone ethylene. Chlorophyll breaks down and the pigment lycopene builds up, which turns the fruit from green to red.
Colour development is best at moderate temperatures. Roughly above 30 degrees Celsius, lycopene formation is reduced and fruit can stay yellow-orange.
Hot weather, water stress and nutrient problems can therefore change how quickly or evenly a field ripens.

# Tomato growth and development stages
Source: University extension crop guides (for example UC ANR, UF/IFAS, Alabama and Oklahoma State extension) and UC Davis Postharvest Technology Center. Timings are approximate. Verify before citing.

## Stages overview
Tomato growth is usually described in stages: germination, seedling, vegetative growth, flowering, fruit set and enlargement, mature green, ripening and senescence. Timing depends on variety, temperature, light and management, so any number of days is only approximate.

## Germination and seedling
Seeds germinate faster in warm soil and slowly in cold soil. Seedlings are moved to the field once they have several true leaves and the weather is warm enough, because tomato plants are damaged by frost.

## Vegetative growth
Stems and leaves grow quickly. Indeterminate varieties keep growing taller. Too much nitrogen at this stage gives lush leaves and can delay flowering.

## Flowering and fruit set
Flowers open in clusters called trusses. A flower must be pollinated to set fruit, and temperature extremes, very high or low humidity, too much nitrogen, low light or a heavy fruit load can make flowers drop. Fruit set is the point at which a pollinated flower swells into a small green fruit.

## Fruit enlargement
After set, the fruit enlarges for several weeks while still green. Uneven watering in this phase raises the risk of cracking and blossom-end rot.

## Mature green stage
When the fruit reaches full size it is called mature green. At this point it can still ripen after picking, because the ripening programme is already in place. Inside, the seed cavities fill with gel. Fruit picked before this point (immature green) does not ripen well.

## Ripening
Ripening starts at the breaker stage, when the first tan, pink or red colour appears, usually at the blossom end. It is driven by the plant hormone ethylene. Chlorophyll breaks down, the red pigment lycopene builds up, sugars rise, the flesh softens and flavour develops. Tomatoes are climacteric fruit, which is why they keep ripening after harvest.

## Ripening across the plant
Fruit on the lowest truss ripens first and later trusses follow. An indeterminate plant therefore carries unripe, partly ripe and ripe fruit at the same time, and a field is rarely uniform. Determinate varieties ripen more of their crop in a shorter window.

## Approximate timing
In moderate weather, the time from flowering to ripe fruit is commonly around 6 to 8 weeks. Warm weather shortens it and cool weather lengthens it. From transplanting to first harvest, roughly 2 to 3 months is common, depending on variety.

## End of season
Determinate plants finish after the main crop. Indeterminate plants continue until frost or disease. Cool nights slow ripening, and frost damages the plants and fruit, so green fruit is often picked before frost and ripened indoors.
